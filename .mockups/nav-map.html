The healthcare switch sets the guardrails

One toggle per business, at the top where the business is chosen. Flick it on and the rules follow the business everywhere — the owner never has to remember.

  • Every post, blog and ad is checked against AHPRA and TGA before it can go out, and a check that cannot run blocks rather than passes.
  • A compliance record appears under Settings — the dated evidence, which is what you would hand a regulator.
  • Wording changes throughout: no outcome claims, no testimonials, no before-and-after.
  • Advertising gains a health-specific section, because the ad rules differ.

Off, and none of that clutter appears. Scent Sell should never see an AHPRA warning.

Most people will have one business design for that

You have fourteen; almost every subscriber will have one. So the business selector is quiet when there is only one — it just shows the name and the healthcare switch. It becomes a real switcher only when there is something to switch between.

Designing the fourteen-business case as the default would make the product feel wrong to the person paying for it.

Connections is the one to get visually right new

It is where the product either feels connected or feels broken, and it is the screen a new subscriber hits first. Big obvious tiles per service, each showing plainly whether it is connected, what NRS can do once it is, and one button to connect it.

An account that has stopped working is the loudest thing on the screen, because a channel that silently cannot publish is the most expensive failure here.
