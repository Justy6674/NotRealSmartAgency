Being found

Two different jobs, and they are fixed in different ways. Google is someone typing a search. AI searchability is someone asking an assistant instead. You need both.

Google searchability · not connected AI searchability · checked 16 Aug
Section one

Google searchability

Not connected

Being found when someone types something like “weight loss clinic Brisbane” into Google.

We cannot see your Google results yet

Google keeps a private record of every search that showed your site — the exact words people typed, where you appeared on the page, and how many clicked. Only you can open that record to us. It takes about a minute. We only read it. We never change your site or your listing.

What you get the moment it is connected
  • ✓The searches you already appear for, and what position you sit at.
  • ✓What people near you are actually typing — in their words, not ours.
  • ✓Whether your Google listing is correct: hours, phone, address, categories.
  • ✓Where you come up in the map results for each suburb you serve.
Opens Google’s own sign-in page. You pick which account, and you can disconnect it any time from Connections.
Your searches, positions and clicks will show here. Empty on purpose. We have not measured any of it, so we are not going to draw you a chart of it.

Why you are behind — and none of this needed Google

We measured all four of these ourselves, from your own website and from your competitors’ public pages. They are true today, connected or not, and they are the reason you are not showing up.

1

Your competitors have 90 blog posts between them. You have 4.

Google hands its answers to whoever has written the most useful page about a question. With four posts, that is almost never you. Juniper has 41, Eucalyptus 32, Mosh 17.

Counted on their websites·14 August
⚖ Anything written for you is checked against AHPRA & TGA rules before you can publish it
2

Your site takes 6.0 seconds to open on a phone. Google starts holding sites back after about 2.5.

Most people who search for you are on a phone on mobile data. Half of them leave before your page appears. The single biggest cause is the picture at the top of your home page: it is 4.2 MB, around twenty times bigger than it needs to be.

Measured on your home page, normal 4G phone connection·16 August
3

Nine of your pages carry the same one-line description.

That line is what Google prints under your name in the results. When nine pages share it, eight of them look like copies and get buried. Each one needs a sentence of its own.

Found in your website scan·16 August
4

You have no page for the places you actually treat people.

People search for a service and a suburb together. You have one page covering all of Australia. Juniper has 46 separate location pages, so they appear and you do not.

Counted on their website·14 August

How AI currently describes you

This is the part that matters most. Being mentioned is no good if what they say is wrong. You are the one who decides whether each of these is right — tick it or flag it.

“Downscale will get you a prescription for weight-loss injections online, no assessment needed. Phone (07) 5550 0198.”

Three things wrong. Advertising a prescription-only treatment like that is against the TGA rules — we could not say it, so we certainly do not want an assistant saying it for us. “No assessment needed” is untrue: every patient is assessed by a nurse practitioner. And that phone number is not yours — it is the old one from your Facebook page.

✗ You flagged this — 16 August
ChatGPT·asked 16 August
No description. It does not know you exist.

Nothing to correct here — there is nothing to correct with. The three fixes below are what changes this one.

Nothing said about you

What to fix

Assistants read your website the way a person skim-reads it: the opening, the headings, and any page that answers a question directly. Three things are stopping them.

1

Your home page never says, in plain words, what you do.

It opens with “Transform your journey”. Nowhere in the first 200 words does it say telehealth, weight loss, Australia, or nurse practitioner. An assistant reading that has nothing to repeat back to someone.

2

There is no page that answers the questions people actually ask.

Assistants quote whichever page answers the question straight out. “How much does it cost”, “do I need a referral”, “is it covered by Medicare” — you have a page for none of them, and your competitors have all three.

⚖ Checked before you publish
3

Your contact details are different in three places.

The phone number on your website, on your Google listing and on your Facebook page do not match. When they disagree, an assistant picks one. That is exactly where Perplexity got the wrong number above.
