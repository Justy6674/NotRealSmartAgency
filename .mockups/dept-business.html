What the business is

Used at the top of nearly everything written about you

Downscale is a telehealth weight-loss clinic. We look after adults anywhere in Australia by phone and video — a first consultation, blood tests through a collection centre near them, scripts sent to the pharmacy they already use, and a review every four weeks. Care is led by endorsed nurse practitioners, with a doctor available for anything outside their scope. We are not a program, a challenge or a meal-replacement service, and we say so early, because most people who find us have already been sold one of those and it ended when the payments did.

Words to use, and words to avoid

Checked on everything before it reaches you

Words to use

Your preference. Add or remove anything — these are style, not law.

clinician nurse practitioner appointment review your plan eligible suitable for you side effects we will check steady at your own pace your GP living with obesity

Words you would rather avoid

Also your preference. Remove any of these whenever you like.

journey guys shed the kilos bikini body cheat day guilt-free just simply obese fat
Words you are not allowed to use Set by law

These are not a style choice. They come from the AHPRA advertising rules and the TGA advertising code, which apply to you because Downscale is a registered health service. Breaking them carries fines of up to $60,000 per offence, so they cannot be deleted from this page. If one is wrong or you need an exception, ask for a review and a person will look at it.

Ozempic · Wegovy · Saxenda · Mounjaro

Brand names of prescription-only medicines. Naming them to the public is an offence, even in a comment reply. Say instead: “prescription treatment”, or “medication your clinician may consider”.

semaglutide · tirzepatide · liraglutide

The ingredient names of the same medicines. The rule follows the medicine, not the spelling.

the jab · the pen · the weight loss injection

Nicknames still point at a prescription-only medicine, so they are treated the same way. This one catches people out most often.

guaranteed · guaranteed results

Creates an expectation of benefit that cannot be promised for a health service.

cure · miracle · transformation

Same rule, and it also describes an outcome no clinician can commit to in advance.

safe · no side effects · risk-free

Understates risk. Misleading under both sets of rules, and it is the sort of line a complaint gets built on.

any “lose X kg in Y weeks” claim

A specific outcome promise. Still not allowed even when a real patient did exactly that.

best · #1 · leading · Australia’s top

Comparative and superlative claims about a health service are not permitted.

before-and-after photos · patient results in their own words

Testimonials about clinical care are prohibited. This covers reviews you share or repost, not only the ones you write.

only 5 spots left · offer ends Friday

A time-limited push to use a health service. Ordinary marketing everywhere else, not allowed here.
